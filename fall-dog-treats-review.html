If your dog's activity or treat routine changes in autumn, review the daily portion. Extra rewards from visitors and food-filled toys can add calories that are easy to overlook.

For most dogs, treats should supply no more than 10% of daily calories. This is a ceiling, not a target. Ask your veterinarian about your dog's calorie needs and any stricter dietary limits. VCA guidance on treat calories

How to calculate a treat portion

  1. Multiply the recommended daily calorie intake by 0.10 to estimate the maximum treat allowance.
  2. Subtract calories from other treats, edible chews, table scraps, and extras added to toys.
  3. Divide the remaining allowance by the kcal per piece on the label. Round down to whole pieces, then follow any lower limit in the feeding directions or your veterinarian's advice.

For example, a recommended intake of 700 kcal gives a maximum treat allowance of 70 kcal. After 20 kcal of other extras, 50 kcal remain. If a treat contains 5 kcal per piece, the calculation allows up to 10 pieces. These numbers are an illustration, not a feeding recommendation for a particular dog or product.

If calories per piece are unavailable, ask the manufacturer before estimating a portion. See our daily treat allowance guide for more on planning portions.

12 常见误区同时覆盖即时风险和长期过量

P1

位置：第343至363行Common Fall Treat Mistakes to Avoid，包括Practical tip。

Most problems are not caused by a single bad treat.

问题：单次误食也可能造成严重问题。“风味不改变热量”过于绝对，配方改变可能改变热量。为增加零食而直接减少主食，可能削弱完整均衡营养；优先减少额外零食更清晰。把一周混合零食称重放一盒也不能代替热量核算。

怎么改：本节标题保留，其余内容整体替换。

Fall treat problems can involve an unsafe food, an unsuitable chew, or repeated overfeeding. Watch for these common mistakes.

  • Choosing by the seasonal label. A pumpkin shape or fall flavor does not establish nutritional quality. Check the recipe and calorie statement.
  • Letting treats replace balanced food. Keep extras within the daily allowance so the main diet remains the source of essential nutrients.
  • Leaving a chew unattended. Stay with your dog and monitor how the chew changes as it is eaten.
  • Letting guests add uncounted rewards. Give visitors a measured portion from the day's allowance.
  • Increasing treats without reviewing the total. Reduce extra treats first. Ask your veterinarian before making substantial changes to meals or a prescribed diet.

Practical tip: Measure one day's treat portion using the calorie information for each product. If your dog repeatedly gains weight, review the full diet and body condition with your veterinarian.

Stop offering it and contact your veterinarian if your dog develops vomiting, diarrhea, or itching. Dogs with medical conditions or prescribed diets need veterinary advice before adding new treats.

Can dogs eat pumpkin pie filling?

Avoid pumpkin pie filling. It can contain added sugar, spices, or other ingredients that are unsuitable for dogs. If pumpkin fits your dog's diet, choose plain cooked pumpkin or 100% canned pumpkin and include it in the treat allowance. Pumpkin guidance

How many fall treats can my dog have per day?

There is no fixed number for every dog. Use no more than 10% of the recommended daily calories for all treats and extras, or a lower limit set by your veterinarian. Subtract other extras, then divide the remaining calories by the kcal per piece on the label. Follow any lower limit in the product's feeding directions. WSAVA treat guidance

Are rawhide-free chews safer than rawhide?

A rawhide-free label alone does not establish that a chew is safer. Size, hardness, and chewing behavior still matter. Supervise your dog and remove a chew before the remaining piece can be swallowed whole. Ask your veterinarian about suitable options for a dog that gulps food or has dental problems.

What autumn foods should dogs avoid?

Keep chocolate, grapes and raisins, xylitol, macadamia nuts, alcohol, onions, and garlic away from dogs. Check mixed dishes such as pies, gravy, and stuffing for these ingredients. Contact a veterinarian or pet poison service promptly after suspected ingestion. Rich foods can also cause digestive upset; do not assume that a small serving is harmless. ASPCA food guidance

Do I need to change my dog's food in autumn?

A new season alone is not a reason to change a diet that suits your dog. If activity, weight, or treat intake changes, review the total daily calories and reduce unnecessary extras. Ask your veterinarian before making substantial changes to meals, especially for a dog on a prescribed diet.
