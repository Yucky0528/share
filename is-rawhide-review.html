The main concerns discussed in veterinary and animal-welfare guidance are choking, digestive obstruction, stomach upset, and tooth injury. These are different risks and should not be treated as one problem.

Choking

A swallowed chew or torn-off piece can obstruct the airway. Difficulty breathing requires immediate emergency veterinary care. Repeated retching also needs urgent attention and should not be assumed to be choking. ASPCA; VCA emergency guidance

Digestive blockage

A swallowed piece may become lodged in the digestive tract. Vomiting, abdominal pain, reduced appetite, lethargy, or difficulty passing stool can indicate a problem that needs veterinary assessment. These signs do not confirm the cause. VCA foreign-body guidance

Digestibility and stomach upset

Rawhide digestibility varies by product and by dog. Large pieces may not break down sufficiently to pass safely. Some dogs also experience digestive upset after eating rawhide. Stop offering the chew and contact your veterinarian if your dog becomes unwell; do not assume symptoms are minor if a piece may have been swallowed. AKC rawhide guidance

Very hard chews can chip or fracture teeth. Ask your veterinarian which textures suit your dog's teeth and chewing behavior, especially if your dog already has dental disease or damaged teeth. AKC edible-chew guidance

Check the product and the dog

Watch what happens during chewing

Is rawhide actually bad for dogs?

Rawhide carries choking and digestive-blockage risks, especially if large pieces are swallowed. It can also cause stomach upset, and very hard chews can damage teeth. Suitability depends on the dog and the specific product. Supervision and appropriate sizing do not eliminate risk.

What are rawhide chews made of?

Rawhide chews are made from processed animal skin. Added ingredients and manufacturing methods vary. Check the complete ingredient list and ask the manufacturer about any missing information. The material name alone does not establish digestibility or safety.

Can puppies have rawhide chews?

VCA advises avoiding rawhide for puppies because of tooth-fracture and swallowed-piece risks. Ask your veterinarian for an age-appropriate alternative. A “for puppies” label or the arrival of adult teeth does not establish that a rawhide chew is suitable for your puppy. VCA puppy guidance

How much rawhide can a dog have per day?

There is no universal daily amount that makes rawhide safe. Treats and edible chews should generally stay within 10% of daily calories, or a lower veterinary limit. This is a calorie budget, not a safe swallowing allowance. Follow the product's directions and your veterinarian's advice; chewing time does not measure the amount consumed. VCA treat guidance

What should I do if my dog swallows a piece of rawhide?

Seek emergency veterinary care for breathing difficulty, repeated unproductive retching, or a suddenly swollen abdomen. For a swallowed large piece or an uncertain piece size, contact your veterinarian promptly even if your dog seems well. Do not attempt home treatment or induce vomiting unless instructed, and do not wait for the piece to pass before calling. VCA emergency guidance; ASPCA chew guidance
